How the model works

Agents. Tumor cells occupy lattice sites (15 µm each) and divide into free space, becoming quiescent when crowded or hypoxic and necrotic when oxygen runs out. Each cell carries heritable antigenicity and PD-L1 that mutate on division, creating new clones. CD8 T cells live on their own layer and can move between tumor cells.

Fields. Oxygen diffuses from vessels, which regress when the tumor engulfs them. Tumor cells secrete a chemokine (more when dying), which recruits T cells at vessels and guides them by chemotaxis, and a suppressor that slows T cells and blunts killing.

Rules. On contact, a T cell kills with probability ∝ antigenicity × (1 − PD-L1 block) × (1 − suppression). Kills drive clonal expansion and exhaustion. Every event is drawn stochastically each 6-minute step.

Anti-PD-1. From the treatment day, the PD-L1 term in the kill probability and the PD-L1-driven exhaustion rate are both reduced by the drug efficacy (90%). T cells that are already fully exhausted are not reinvigorated.
